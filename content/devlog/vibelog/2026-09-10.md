## What I did

Each day's short video now looks different from the one before. This blog reads my projects' [[commits|saved checkpoints of work, one chunk at a time]] every night and publishes a post plus a 30–45 second [[short|a brief vertical video]] on its own. Until now, every video ran in the same order with the same screens.

Today I split the stories into 3 shapes: a day I shipped something, a day I fixed something broken, and a day that compares yesterday with today. Each video picks a different shape from the last one. The first 2 seconds open with that day's commit list being typed out. When the narration says a number, the number counts up in the middle of the screen. Each project can also pick one of 5 themes with its own colors and mood. I also touched up the captions and thumbnails.

## Why

If a daily video looks identical from the first frame, there's less reason to watch. Scenes just went by one after another, so nothing grabbed attention in the first few seconds. Numbers also slipped past as spoken words only.

## Rabbit holes

The first scene of each video shows that day's story as one big line. As the narration reads it, the key words get colored in for emphasis. But in yesterday's English video, not a single word got colored. It turned out that when the key phrase came at the end of a sentence, a period was stuck to it. So the program that makes the video treated "started." as different from the key word "started" and skipped it. I changed it to ignore punctuation at either end of a word. The Korean videos had the same problem. It just showed up less, because key phrases there usually land mid-sentence.
